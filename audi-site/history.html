1909

August Horch Founds Audi

After leaving his first company (Horch), August Horch establishes Audi Automobilwerke GmbH in Zwickau, Germany. The name "Audi" is the Latin translation of "Horch" (meaning "listen").

1932

Auto Union Is Born

Four brands — Audi, DKW, Horch, and Wanderer — merge to form Auto Union AG. The iconic four-ring logo is created, representing the unity of these four Saxon manufacturers.

1965

Volkswagen Acquisition

Volkswagen Group acquires Auto Union from Daimler-Benz. The Audi name is revived for a new generation of vehicles, beginning with the Audi 72 (later renamed Audi 80).

1969

NSU Merger

Auto Union merges with NSU Motorenwerke to form Audi NSU Auto Union AG. NSU brings rotary engine expertise and the legendary Ro 80 to the portfolio.

1980

The quattro Revolution

Audi unveils the quattro — the first all-wheel-drive production car. It dominates rally racing, winning four World Rally Championship titles and changing automotive engineering forever.

1994

Audi A8 — Aluminum Revolution

The first generation A8 debuts with the Audi Space Frame — an all-aluminum body that reduces weight while increasing rigidity. It establishes Audi in the luxury segment.

2006

R8 — The Supercar

Audi launches the R8 mid-engine supercar. Sharing DNA with the Le Mans-winning R8 race car, it proves Audi can compete with Ferrari and Porsche.

2018

e-tron — Electric Era Begins

The Audi e-tron becomes the brand's first fully electric production vehicle. 400 hp, 400 km range, and 150 kW charging mark Audi's commitment to an electric future.

2026

All-Electric Future

Audi commits to launching only fully electric models from 2026 onward, with the last internal combustion engine vehicle rolling off the line by 2033. The future is electric.
